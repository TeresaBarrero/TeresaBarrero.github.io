## The concept

<p class="az-quote">Smell first. Seville follows.</p>

Every single-origin coffee comes with an aroma from the land that goes with it. This is the **aroma pairing**, the ritual that defines the brand, and it works just as well at the table as to go.

<ol class="az-steps">
<li><b>Choose your coffee</b><span>Single origins from Ethiopia, Kenya, Colombia or Brazil, depending on the season.</span></li>
<li><b>Smell its aroma</b><span>The coffee arrives with a small ceramic pot holding the aroma of the season.</span></li>
<li><b>Drink</b><span>The aroma prepares the palate: the coffee tastes different. To go, an aroma tab does the ritual in ten seconds.</span></li>
</ol>

Seville’s **scent calendar** shapes the year: the menu, the shop’s aroma and the take-home products change four times, and each change of season works as a relaunch of the brand.

<div class="az-seasons">
<div><span>Spring · March to May</span><b>Orange blossom</b><small>Washed Ethiopian</small></div>
<div><span>Summer · June to September</span><b>Jasmine and mint</b><small>Kenyan, also as cold brew</small></div>
<div><span>Autumn · October and November</span><b>Quince and chestnut</b><small>Colombian or Guatemalan</small></div>
<div><span>Winter · December to February</span><b>Bitter orange and cinnamon</b><small>Brazilian or Peruvian</small></div>
</div>

The story gives it roots: for two centuries Seville was the Port of the Indies, the gateway through which the world entered Europe. AZARA takes back that role, with coffees from many origins arriving at an Andalusian patio. The name comes from *azahar*, the blossom of Seville’s orange trees.

## Audience

<div class="az-personas">
<div><b>Lucía, 31</b><em>The urban explorer</em><p>A designer in Madrid. She finds places on Instagram and TikTok and is tired of specialty coffee shops that all look the same.</p><span>AZARA gives her a different ritual every season and a space worth photographing.</span></div>
<div><b>Javier, 48</b><em>The Andalusian away from home</em><p>From Seville, working in Madrid, he misses the smell of orange blossom. Kitsch and Andalusian clichés put him off.</p><span>AZARA gives him an authentic, modern Andalusia and a takeaway coffee made with the same care.</span></div>
<div><b>Emma, 42</b><em>The cultural traveller</em><p>A British tourist on a short break. She wants local experiences and can’t tell whether a place is genuine or a tourist trap.</p><span>AZARA gives her an experience with a story and products to take the season home.</span></div>
</div>

## Identity

The logo is a **coffee tile**: a Triana tile with a Mudéjar interlaced star and, in the centre, an orange blossom whose five petals are coffee beans. It has a main version and a compact one for seals, cup lids and icons.

The visual concept, **“The Orange Blossom Patio”**, guides the photography: golden light, whitewash, terracotta, handmade ceramics, tiles and real oranges. The voice has two registers: poetic for the brand (“Smell first. Seville follows.”) and warm on social media and in person (“The orange blossom is here. We’re saving your first spring coffee.”). No flamenco frills, bulls or clichés.

## Strategy

Differentiation through experience and identity, never price, in four phases. The pre-launch runs through the bitter orange season and the opening, on 4 March, lands with the orange blossom, the season that gives the brand its name.

<div class="az-phases">
<div><span>December to February</span><b>Pre-launch</b><p>Instagram and TikTok with the story and the shop being built, a weekend pop-up to try the ritual and a waiting list.</p></div>
<div><span>March</span><b>Opening</b><p>“The orange blossom is here” week, creators and press, the Google profile and the online shop launch.</p></div>
<div><span>April to February</span><b>Consolidation</b><p>A campaign every season, a four-season loyalty programme, monthly tastings and a subscription.</p></div>
<div><span>Year 2</span><b>Expansion</b><p>Brand and operations manual, corners in hotels and shops, and a study for the second shop.</p></div>
</div>

On the digital side, the plan includes the website with the ritual, the scent calendar and the shop; a calendar of 53 Instagram and Facebook posts from December to March, built on five pillars (brand, origin, ritual, season and community); the Google Business Profile with its description, products, first posts and model replies to reviews; and a newsletter at every change of season.

## Context

AZARA Coffee is a brand concept project developed for my portfolio: it is not a real business. The coffee would be roasted by El Hombre Pez, a real roaster in Seville, but the partnership is fictional. The images are provisional and were generated with AI (Gemini) from the project’s art direction. The social media pieces and the loyalty card were designed using the copy from the social media calendar and the plan.
